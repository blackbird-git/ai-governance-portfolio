AI governance and risk assessment

SafeGuard

A way to check what could go wrong when a business connects AI to its real systems.

Why I started it

Imagine a company adds an AI assistant that can read customer records and update information in another application. The assistant might give excellent answers, but that doesn't tell us whether it has access to too much information, whether its actions need approval, or what happens if it makes a mistake.

Those are the questions SafeGuard is designed to answer. Rather than reviewing the AI model in isolation, it looks at the whole connected workflow and the business risks around it.

How it works

The review starts by mapping the people, applications, data, and permissions involved. It examines six areas: ownership, identity and access, data boundaries, approved actions, integration security, and monitoring and recovery.

For each area, the assessment asks what should happen and what evidence shows that it actually happens. That might mean reviewing an access configuration, examining an audit record, or running a limited authorized test. A control that hasn't been verified is recorded as unknown, not simply marked as safe.

The method draws on NIST AI RMF 1.0, NIST CSF 2.0, and the Generative AI Profile. These frameworks help organize the questions and findings; using them does not mean a system has been certified or declared compliant.

What exists today

Published a written assessment methodology and example reports for fictional accounting, healthcare, and energy organizations. They demonstrate how evidence becomes a finding, a recommendation, and a decision someone can act on.

Software to help collect evidence, review findings, and produce reports is still in development. The examples are illustrative, not completed customer engagements.
