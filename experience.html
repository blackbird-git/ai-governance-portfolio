SS&C Technologies

Enterprise systems, technical consulting, and customer delivery

I spent fifteen years at SS&C, starting in enterprise application support and eventually moving into infrastructure engineering and customer-facing technical consulting. I worked with IT teams at banks, pension funds, healthcare organizations, and public agencies. Much of my job involved untangling complicated problems in systems that customers depended on every day, then finding a way to make changes without disrupting their operations.

One of the larger efforts was modernizing approximately 150 Linux systems across customer-hosted environments, SS&C's private cloud, AWS, and Azure. The operating system upgrade was only part of it. I had to work through application dependencies, certificates, integrations, testing, and the practical questions around scheduling and validating changes in production.

Identity and security were regular parts of that work. I configured and troubleshot single sign-on with Okta, Microsoft Entra ID, and ADFS, used SAML traces to investigate authentication problems, and worked with TLS certificates and IPsec connections. I also supported customer escalations and security reviews, where the challenge was often explaining a technical issue clearly enough for different teams to decide what to do about it.

I helped replace Nagios monitoring with Instana, reducing false alerts by roughly 90% and cutting investigation effort by about half. I was also responsible for VMware Site Recovery Manager disaster recovery readiness, testing, and cutovers. Those projects reinforced something I came to value: good technical work is not just getting a system running. It is being able to verify it, recover it, and explain its risks to the people relying on it.

Why AI governance

Much of my career has been spent translating requirements into systems that actually work—and explaining the risks when they don't. AI adds new failure modes, but many of the practical questions are familiar: who is responsible, what can the system access, how is it tested, and what happens when something goes wrong?

That's why my independent projects focus on evidence, controls, and accountable decisions, rather than treating a framework or a passing test as proof that a system is safe. I'm interested in roles where I can help teams put those principles into practice.
