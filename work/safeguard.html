SafeGuard

I designed SafeGuard to assess the risks of adopting and integrating AI systems. It connects system access, data handling, security controls, and supporting evidence to findings a business can prioritize and act on. I published illustrative assessments and am developing software to support the process.

Background

AI integration reviews often rely on vendor assurances, incomplete architecture details, and security claims that have not been tested. I built SafeGuard to organize the questions that matter, identify missing evidence, and connect each finding to an actionable decision.

The method records what the system does, which risks apply, what controls are expected, how they were evaluated, and what the organization needs to change.

Approach

I developed the review method and wrote illustrative reports first, so the assessment process would stand on its own. The software under development is intended to make evidence handling, consistency checks, and human review more repeatable.

Establish scope

Identify the AI use case, data touched, integration boundaries, intended users, and meaningful business consequences.

Evaluate controls

Use NIST AI RMF, its Generative AI Profile, and NIST CSF 2.0 as reference frameworks for evaluating security and governance controls. Framework alignment is not the same as proof of compliance.

Preserve evidence

Connect findings to the observations or documents that support them, and distinguish known facts from assumptions or missing information.

Recommend action

Prioritize findings by severity, describe business impact, and propose feasible mitigations with human review of conclusions.

My contribution: I defined the assessment method, control mapping, risk reporting structure, and illustrative deliverables. I also direct development of supporting software for evidence management and report review.

Results and evidence

I produced three illustrative assessments for fictional organizations in accounting, healthcare, and energy. Each demonstrates how I scope an AI use case, identify control gaps, and turn documented evidence into prioritized recommendations.

These are examples of the method, not client engagements or completed production assessments.

Limitations

  • The sample assessments are illustrative; they do not demonstrate client adoption, operational effectiveness, or independent audit certification.
  • Assessment quality still depends on the completeness and trustworthiness of supplied evidence.
  • Framework mappings are interpretation aids, not assurances that a system is legally compliant or risk-free.
  • Software functions should be represented as implemented only after they are tested and validated.

What I learned

Developing the reports made the evaluation criteria and evidence gaps concrete. It also exposed where a reviewer must distinguish a vendor claim from a verified control.

I designed the findings around decisions: who owns a risk, what should change, and what evidence will show the change worked.
